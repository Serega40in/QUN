# QUN Mauritius — user discovery guide

Purpose: discover recurring needs before choosing a product. This is a template; no user interviews have been completed or claimed.

## Who to interview
Initially aim for 5–10 short conversations across small local businesses, residents who buy/sell locally, creators/freelancers, and SME digital leads/developers. A small convenience sample is directional only, not representative of Mauritius.

## Consent script
“We are researching how people in Mauritius currently handle [specific task]. This is research, not a sales pitch. Participation is voluntary; you may skip questions or stop at any time. We will summarize feedback without your name unless you separately agree otherwise. May I take anonymized notes?”

Do not collect identity documents, credentials, payment data, or unnecessary personal details. Ask permission before recording or quoting. Anonymize notes.

## Behaviour-first questions
1. Tell me about the last time you needed to [complete this task]. What did you do, step by step?
2. Which apps, websites, people or offline methods did you use?
3. What was slow, costly, frustrating or risky? Can you describe a recent example?
4. How often does this happen, and what is the impact in time or money?
5. What workaround do you use today?
6. What alternatives have you tried? Why did you continue or stop?
7. Which language works best for this task—English, French, Kreol Morisien, or another—and does it vary by context?
8. Which device/network conditions do you usually have? Any accessibility needs?
9. What makes you distrust a new service? What support or safety controls would you expect?
10. What outcome would make the problem meaningfully better?

## Business-specific prompts
- How do customers currently discover your products/services?
- Where do enquiries arrive, and how do you track and respond to them?
- What are the biggest problems with stock, pricing, delivery, payments, returns, fraud or repeat customers?
- Which tools are essential? What do they cost?
- What would you stop using if a new tool worked well?
- Would you test a prototype with sample or public business information? What would block adoption?

## Resident-specific prompts
- How do you judge whether a listing/seller is trustworthy?
- How do you compare prices and arrange pickup/delivery?
- What happens when an order or transaction goes wrong?

## Developer/creator prompts
- Which repetitive tasks consume the most time?
- Which APIs, integrations or AI workflows are hard to use locally?
- What documentation, governance or contribution workflow would make you trust an open ecosystem?

## Avoid leading questions
Do not start with “Would you use QUN?” or “Do you like our idea?” Do not present speculative features as already available. If showing a prototype, ask participants to complete a task and observe where they struggle.

## Synthesis
Count distinct participants, not comments. Record contradictory and negative evidence. Do not generalize from a small convenience sample to the whole country. Choose a pilot only when several independent participants describe the same recent problem and are willing to test a concrete solution.
