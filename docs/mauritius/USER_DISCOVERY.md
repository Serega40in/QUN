# QUN Mauritius — user discovery guide

Purpose: discover real needs before selecting a QUN Mauritius MVP. This is a template; no interviews have yet been claimed or fabricated.

## Recruitment targets
Aim for 5–10 short interviews initially, spread across:
- small retailers and informal/home businesses;
- residents who buy or sell locally;
- creators/freelancers;
- developers or people responsible for digital tools at SMEs.

Do not collect identity documents, account credentials, payment data or unnecessary personal information. Ask permission before taking notes or quoting. Anonymize findings.

## Opening and consent
“We are researching how people in Mauritius currently handle [selling locally / finding customers / business content / digital tools]. This is research, not a sales pitch. Participation is voluntary. You can skip any question or stop at any time. We will summarize feedback without your name unless you explicitly agree otherwise. May I take anonymized notes?”

## Questions — ask about behaviour first
1. Tell me about the last time you needed to [complete the relevant task]. What did you do first, and what happened next?
2. Which apps, websites, people or offline methods did you use?
3. What was frustrating, slow, costly or risky? Can you give a recent example?
4. How often does this happen? What does it cost in time or money?
5. What workaround do you use today?
6. What have you already tried, and why did you stop or continue?
7. Which languages do you prefer for this task: English, French, Kreol Morisien, or another language? Does the answer change by context?
8. What device and network conditions do you usually use? Any accessibility needs?
9. What would make you distrust a new service? What safety or support would you expect?
10. What would a better outcome look like, and how would you judge whether it worked?

## Role-specific prompts
### Local business
- How do customers discover products today?
- Where do enquiries arrive, and how are they tracked?
- What are the biggest issues with stock, delivery, payment, returns, fraud or repeat customers?
- Which existing tools are essential? What fees do you pay, if any?

### Resident / buyer / seller
- How do you judge whether a seller or listing is trustworthy?
- How do you compare prices and arrange handover?
- What happens when something goes wrong?

### Creator / developer / SME digital lead
- Which tasks consume repetitive time?
- Which tools or APIs are hard to access or integrate?
- Would language adaptation, workflow automation or a shared identity solve a recurring problem?

## Avoid leading questions
Do not ask “Would you use QUN?” or “Do you like our idea?” before understanding current behaviour. Do not present speculative features as already available. If showing a prototype, ask the participant to complete a task and observe where they struggle.

## Synthesis
After each batch, group repeated problems. Count distinct participants, not number of comments. Record negative evidence and contradictions. Do not generalize from a tiny convenience sample to the whole country.
